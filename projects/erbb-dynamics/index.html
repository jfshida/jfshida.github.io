ErbB receptor dynamics.
Microscope to analysis.

Connecting a custom imaging instrument with quantitative analysis to understand receptor interactions in living cells.

Co-first author

My contribution

I was responsible for all data analysis and microscope construction for this study. My work connected the experimental platform with the quantitative interpretation of the measurements.

The scientific question

When do receptors
find a partner?

The study followed EGFR, HER2, and HER3 in living cells to resolve dimer formation, dissociation, and the effects of oncogenic mutations.

Instrument + computation

Two parts of
one measurement.

01

Building the microscope

The custom instrument combined 976 nm excitation, switchable epi/TIRF illumination, four-channel detection, active focus stabilization, and a controlled live-cell environment.

  • Optical integration
  • Multichannel detection
  • Focus stability
02

Analyzing the data

The workflow used SLIMfast2 and Piscis/ByteTrack for trajectories, Bayesian hidden Markov models for diffusion states, GPU computation, and dimer-lifetime analysis in Python and MATLAB.

  • Python / MATLAB
  • Bayesian inference
  • GPU computation

Methods summarized from the paper’s STAR Methods.

Study outcome

Interactions,
resolved over time.

The measurements revealed stable ligand-independent EGFR mutant dimers and constitutive HER2/HER3 homodimers, helping resolve how mutations alter receptor interactions.

Validation + limitations

Accounting for imperfect tracks

Manual trajectory review and truncation simulations checked robustness. Crowded spots and out-of-focus motion remained limitations; sparse labeling made measured dimer fractions lower bounds.

Relevance to autonomous experimentation

This project demonstrates the connection between instrument development and quantitative interpretation—a foundation for building experimental systems whose decisions depend on reliable measurements.
